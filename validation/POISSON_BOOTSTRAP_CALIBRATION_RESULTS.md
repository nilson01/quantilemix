# Marginal Poisson row-bootstrap known-score study

The reviewed `poisson-bootstrap-known-scores-v1` study measured the repeated-dataset behavior of the implemented normal interval from `fit_poisson_bootstrap` with 200 row resamples. Its target was the marginal log mean ratio for a model-based change of all four known exposure scores from 0 to 1. The model-based `fit_poisson` interval was a descriptive comparison on the same datasets. Fixed-input agreement with installed R qgcomp 2.19.6 is reported separately in [POISSON_BOOTSTRAP_RESULTS.md](POISSON_BOOTSTRAP_RESULTS.md) and does not establish interval coverage.

Each of four prespecified conditions used 1,000 independent datasets of 500 rows. Four scores were independent uniform draws from 0–3, and a binary adjustment covariate was independent of them. Conditional counts followed the correctly specified additive Poisson-log model in the [protocol](POISSON_BOOTSTRAP_PROTOCOL.md). The center-point mean (m) sets the mean at score vector (1.5, 1.5, 1.5, 1.5) and adjustment value zero. Because the adjustment factor cancels in the ratio, the true marginal log mean ratio is the sum of the four score coefficients, (b). The actual average count differs from (m), especially because the adjustment covariate increases the mean.

All 4,000 planned datasets completed. Both methods succeeded on all 4,000, with no captured warnings or missing cases. Thus each reported coverage fraction is also its all-planned coverage bound. Bias is average estimate minus true (b), with Monte Carlo standard error (MCSE) in parentheses. Each coverage estimate has a cellwise 95% Wilson interval for the fraction across 1,000 datasets. The table does not adjust these intervals for multiple cells or methods.

The bootstrap interval covered the true effect in 95.2%–96.0% of datasets across these four conditions. No bias exceeded three MCSEs. All Wilson intervals include 0.95, and none lies wholly outside the prespecified 0.93–0.97 review band. These are observations under the specified design, not an equivalence test or proof of exact 95% coverage. The root mean reported bootstrap variance corresponds to standard errors 0.1645, 0.1627, 0.0512 and 0.0505 in table order; empirical estimate SDs were 0.1587, 0.1620, 0.0498 and 0.0479. The compact [summary JSON](poisson-bootstrap-calibration-study-summary.json) retains full precision, all planned/successful denominators, failures, variance measures and Wilson intervals for rejection.

For the two null conditions, bootstrap rejection at (p<0.05) was 0.041 [0.030, 0.055] and 0.048 [0.036, 0.063]. Here rejection is the complement of interval coverage for the same two-sided normal test; it is not a second independent validation. At true (b=0.40), rejection was 0.717 [0.688, 0.744] and 1.000 [0.996, 1.000], which are descriptive power estimates. The model-based interval covered 95.4%–96.3% across the same four cells. These small observed differences do not establish that either variance method is better.

The earlier conditional Poisson-log study found 93.4% coverage in its sparse null cell. That study used a different interval, no adjustment covariate and a lower realized average count. Its result and this marginal-bootstrap result therefore cannot be compared as a controlled method contrast. Both remain visible as separate evidence.

This result applies to independent, prequantized known scores, equal observation periods, one binary adjustment covariate, a correctly specified Poisson mean/variance and the implemented 200-resample normal interval. It does not establish coverage for raw-exposure cutpoint estimation, ties, correlated or mixed-sign exposures, overdispersion, offsets, weights, clusters, missingness, nonlinear or effect-modified marginal contrasts, or other outcome families. In this additive model the adjustment factor cancels from the ratio, so the study does not test uncertainty unique to noncancelling standardization. It does not support a causal interpretation or full R-package coverage.
