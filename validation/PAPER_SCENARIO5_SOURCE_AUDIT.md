# Scenario 5 and Figure 1: source audit

The question is whether the published nine-exposure Figure 1 can be reproduced exactly from the public paper and code. **It cannot be replayed exactly from the public materials inspected here.** This is a source gap, not a failed Python fit. The existing [18,000-fit study](PAPER_SCENARIO5_RESULTS.md) is a checked qgcomp-only translation and should keep that name.

## What the sources specify

The [paper's Table 1 and Figure 1](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7228100/) specify 500 observations, nine exposure scores, three requested correlations (0, 0.4, 0.75), three coefficient pairs (0.25 with −0.20, −0.10, or −0.05), and 1,000 datasets per setting. The true joint effects from those coefficients are 0.05, 0.15, and 0.20. Figure 1 compares quantile g-computation with weighted quantile sum (WQS) regression using bias boxplots. The paper identifies qgcomp 1.3 and gWQS 2.0 defaults for its simulations. Its scenario-5 prose gives 0.25 for the largest joint effect, but the coefficient pair, Table 1, and Figure 1 caption give 0.20. The coefficient sum is the consistent target for our translated study.

We downloaded the [official supplementary bundle](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC7228100/supplementaryFiles) again and inspected its nested code ZIP without running it. The nested ZIP SHA-256 is `ba2a8a858a31f24593d38d94dd204db2a18a214b4aa61645cf7748f988f54ccd`, matching the earlier protocol. It contains only `qgcomp_analysis.R` (SHA-256 `b26f2c058aa87315c43127eab163448aaa12ebd5cb72ee8c80a6405f20530267`) and `qgcomp_simdata.R` (SHA-256 `4e4a9dcad1d2f27cdbdf06c06e5308711d8a443b6ec4cc132ef176a93f66b9e2`).

The generator in `qgcomp_simdata.R` supplies a balanced 0–3 score construction. Its `ncor=2` branch applies the shared-master construction to columns 1 **through 3**, not only to the two causal exposures. The released scenario-5 call uses `coef=c(0.25,-0.05,0,0)`, `corr=0.75`, and `ncor=2`: four exposures and one of the paper's nine settings. `qgcomp_analysis.R` sets `Niter=5` and says these results are not the article's 1,000-run results. Its `analysis_1_5` function fixes exposure names to `x1`–`x4` for both qgcomp and WQS. Increasing the generator's coefficient vector to nine values would therefore still leave five exposures out of that analysis unless the function were changed.

## What is missing and what follows

The inspected release does not contain the nine-exposure Figure 1 production script, its complete random streams or case-level estimates, or a numeric table of its boxplot values. The article and sample code do not resolve whether the production generator correlated the third, noncausal exposure. Nor do they provide the exact nine-exposure WQS call and random state. A search of the public author/package sources found no separate Figure 1 production artifact; that search cannot prove that no private or later source exists. We will not infer exact reproduction from a similar-looking plot.

The existing [protocol](PAPER_SCENARIO5_PROTOCOL.md) therefore made two explicit generator translations: the released `ncor=2` interpretation and an `ncor=1` version that applies the shared-master mechanism only to the two causal exposures. Its [results](PAPER_SCENARIO5_RESULTS.md) cover 1,000 datasets in each of nine coefficient/correlation cells for both versions, with no fit failures; same-input fits agree with installed R qgcomp 1.3.0 and 2.19.6, and an independent QR checker covered every fit. One primary cell had 93.1% observed interval coverage. These are valid findings for the named Python study, but they do not recover the article's Figure 1 data or its WQS comparison. Repeating the same translated study would not close that source gap.

**Decision:** retain the completed translation as the current scenario-5 evidence and leave exact Figure 1 reproduction open. A new paired qgcomp–WQS comparison could answer a different, prospective question if its generator interpretation, WQS version/options, random streams, metrics, failures, and compute plan are fixed first. It must be labeled a new comparison, not an exact replay. For the paper-coverage roadmap, a source-backed qgcomp-only study of the released nonlinear scenarios 7–8 is a more direct next candidate because their generator terms and model formulas are present in the same supplement; that would still need its own reviewed protocol before a material run.
